🧠 Fact-Finding Questions

Phases of Wealth: Accumulation → Management → Distribution. Use the general questions first, then drop into the account type that comes up.

General Questions

At this point in your life, is it more important for you to grow your income, or to defer some of your taxes?

What's your biggest concern about retirement and your estate?

What do you have in place that gives you income no matter what happens with the market?

Do you understand the tax implications of your qualified accounts (IRAs, 401K, TSA, SEP, etc.) to your children?

If you knew right now that your family may only receive 60–75% of your assets when you pass away, would that be of concern or bother you?

401K

When was the last time someone sat with you to match your investment goals with your risk tolerance and time horizon?

What type of match does the plan give you?

How many different investment options does the plan offer? Have they changed over the years?

What expenses are embedded in those funds?

Are you aware of the in-force rollover options or restrictions the plan sponsor has?

If we could secure a portion of your 401K from loss, which portion would that be?

Did your advisor run a distribution table for you to show when you'll run out of money?

What will you do with those taxable RMDs when the time comes?

IRA

Do you know the 4 types of tax-free money (Life Insurance, Roth IRA, 529 plan, Muni Bonds)? If you had the choice, how much of your money would you like to have tax-free?

If the IRS did not require you to take your distribution from your IRA each year, would you still take it?

Mutual Funds

How have the fund managers performed relative to their benchmarks?

What fees are deducted from your account for these funds?

Stock

How often are you being advised on the risk on these investments?

How often is your portfolio being rebalanced and adjusted to meet your risk tolerance?

Variable Annuity

What are the Mortality & Expense & Administrative fees you pay?

Are there any riders you're also paying for?

What are the income withdrawals, death benefits, or other enhancements in your policy?

What do you do with the interest from your annuities?

CD

Do you need the interest, or use the interest, from your CDs to live on?

When does your CD mature? When it does, what do you plan to do with it?

💡 Top 1% Approach

Focus less on products, more on the emotional trigger that creates urgency.

#1 Reason People Buy Insurance

They don't want their family to suffer financially because of a health event, long-term care event, or death. People rarely buy insurance because of features — they buy because they realize: "Someone I love could be affected if I don't have a plan."

Top Humanized Triggers

Trigger #1 — Independence
"Mr./Mrs. Prospect, most people I meet tell me they never want to become a burden on their children. Have you ever thought about what would happen if you needed help for several years?"
Why it works: no selling · creates self-reflection · independence is a stronger motivator than money.

Trigger #2 — Family Protection
"If something happened tomorrow, who would be responsible for handling things financially?"
Why it works: makes the risk personal · creates emotional ownership.

Trigger #3 — Long-Term Care Reality
"Most retirees worry about running out of money. Very few realize a care event can create that problem much faster than market losses."
Why it works: connects retirement and health · opens the LTC conversation naturally.

Trigger #4 — Control
"The people who plan early usually have more choices. The people who wait often have fewer options and higher costs."
Why it works: avoids fear tactics · creates urgency through logic.

Once They Show Need → Best Plan Discussion

If Independence Trigger Hits — talk about Long-Term Care solutions, life insurance with living benefits, asset protection strategies.
Transition: "It sounds like staying independent is important to you. Would it make sense to look at a plan designed to protect your retirement savings if care was ever needed?"

If Family Protection Trigger Hits — talk about Whole Life, Final Expense, Indexed Universal Life (where appropriate and compliant).
Transition: "Based on what you've shared, the goal isn't really insurance. It's making sure your family isn't left with the financial burden."

If Income Protection Trigger Hits — talk about Annuities, guaranteed income strategies, retirement protection.
Transition: "What concerns you more: market losses or outliving your income?"

Top Objection Handling

"I Need To Think About It"
"I completely understand. Usually when someone says they need to think about it, they're thinking about one of three things: the need, the company, or the cost. Which one would be most helpful for us to discuss?"

"It's Too Expensive"
"Compared to what? The monthly premium or the potential cost if the problem actually occurs?" (then pause)

"I Already Have Coverage"
"That's great. Most of my clients did too. The reason they met with me was not to replace coverage but to see if there were gaps they weren't aware of."

"I Need To Talk To My Family"
"Absolutely. They should be involved. What part do you think they'll want the most clarity on so I can help you prepare for that conversation?"

The Top 1% Closing Question

Instead of: ❌ "Would you like to buy this?"
Ask: ✅ "Based on everything we've discussed, does having no plan or having this plan put you in a better position?"

If they answer honestly, many will close themselves.

Simple Formula

Trigger → Story → Need Discovery → Solution → Objection Handling → Decision Question

The highest-performing agents spend roughly 70% of the time asking questions and listening, and 30% discussing solutions — that keeps the conversation human, consultative, and centered on the client's goals.

🍽️ Kitchen-Table Master Cheat Sheet

Strictly agent use only.

1. The 1% Top Reason Clients Buy

Peace of Mind & Asset Protection: Clients don't buy insurance policies for the contract — they buy to preserve their legacy, age with dignity, maintain independence, and keep hard-earned savings intact. Crucially, they buy so their children never have to spend their own savings or alter their personal lifestyles to pay for medical care, extended nursing, or funeral debts.

2. Top Humanized Triggers (Conversational Probes)
Quadrant / FocusVerbatim probe
Universal Core Trigger"If an unexpected health or retirement event happened tomorrow, what would your family or children have to give up financially or personally to help take care of you?"
Retirement / Assisted Care"If you ever needed extended care at home or in a facility, what role do you see your children playing, and how would you feel if that care drained your savings or their college funds for the grandkids?"
Final Expenses / Legacy"Would you feel better knowing your actions today completely remove the financial and emotional burden of funeral debts from your children's shoulders?"
Health Expenses / Medicare"If your health plan didn't cover a major medical gap or hospital bill, which account would you have to pull money from first?"
Retirement Income / Annuities"If the market took a sudden downturn tomorrow, how would an unexpected loss to your nest egg affect your daily retirement lifestyle?"
3. The Solution Bridge & Triplicate of Choice

Step A — The Solution Bridge: "Mr. and Mrs. [Name], after listening to your answers on the Fact Finder, it seems [Stated Priority, e.g., protecting your home / leaving a clear legacy] is your primary concern. If I could show you a way to eliminate that worry in a manner that fits comfortably within your monthly budget, would that interest you?"

Step B — The Triplicate of Choice Presentation: Present an Option Sheet with 3 clear choices to shift the prospect's decision from "Should I buy?" to "Which option fits my budget best?"

4. The 4-Step Objection Reframing Framework

1. Validate — Empathy & Calm: "I can completely appreciate you saying that, Mr. Jones. Many people feel the exact same way when first looking at these figures."

2. Permission — Engage amicably: "May I ask you a quick question about that?" or "I was wondering if we might explore that for just a moment?"

3. Reframe — Fact Finder emotional recall: "Jim, Mary, let me take you back to what you told me earlier when we discussed remaining independent and ensuring your daughter Ann wouldn't have to pay out-of-pocket for your care. You mentioned that keeping your assets intact for each other was your highest priority so your children wouldn't have to spend their own savings. Has something caused you to feel differently now than just a few moments ago?"

4. Action Close — Advocate & close: "You've made good decisions all your life to provide for your family. Have you ever felt bad for making the right decision at the right time for the right reasons? This is one of those times. In order to get this started, what is the correct spelling of your beneficiary's full name?"

Field summary: always address objections on an emotional level rather than an informational level. Re-stating facts creates a battle of wits; repeating their verbatim Fact Finder emotional motives positions you as their trusted advocate.

🏥 "I Don't Have Medicare" Script

Mandatory compliance notice: Bankers Life, Washington National, and their agents are not connected with or endorsed by the U.S. government or the federal Medicare program. When discussing Medicare options, agents MUST provide "A Guide to Health Insurance for People with Medicare" (Form 6862).

Step 1 — Fact Finder Discovery & Validation

Agent (Fact Finder): "John, under the Medical Expenses section of our Fact Finder, what type of primary health insurance do you currently have in place right now?"

Prospect: "Well, Bhupesh, I actually don't have Medicare."

Agent (Validate & Probe): "I completely appreciate you sharing that with me, John. Many people I sit down with are in transition or have unique setups. May I ask what coverage you currently rely on today?"

Scenario A — Turning 65 Soon (Initial Enrollment Period)

Prospect: "I'm turning 65 in a few months, so I haven't signed up yet."

Agent script: "That makes complete sense! You are entering your Initial Enrollment Period — a 7-month window (3 months before, your birthday month, and 3 months after). Signing up on time protects you from lifelong Part B penalties (10% per 12-month delay) and Part D drug penalties. While Original Medicare covers hospital and medical care, it leaves out-of-pocket gaps like the Part A hospital deductible ($1,676) and 20% Part B co-insurance with no cap. If I could show you how a Bankers Life plan fills those gaps within your budget, would that interest you?"

Scenario B — Working Past 65 (Active Group Employer Plan)

Prospect: "I'm 67, but I'm still working and covered under my company's group health plan."

Agent script: "That is very common today! Because you have coverage based on active employment, you qualify for an 8-month Special Enrollment Period (SEP) when you retire without penalty. (Note: COBRA or retiree plans do not qualify as active employment). Many retirees find that transitioning to Original Medicare with a Bankers Life Supplement offers broader doctor choice and lower out-of-pocket costs than group plans. When you prepare to retire, would you like us to review a side-by-side comparison?"

Scenario C — Under 65 / Not Eligible for Medicare

Prospect: "I'm only 58, so I'm nowhere near Medicare age."

Agent script: "I completely understand. Medicare is designed for age 65+ or specific qualifying disabilities. Since Medicare isn't part of your immediate picture, our focus today is protecting your income and accumulated savings from non-Medicare healthcare risks before you retire:

• Assisted Care / Short-Term Care: standard health plans do not cover extended in-home or facility care.
• Critical Illness Cash Benefits: cancer, stroke, or heart attacks create non-medical expense gaps.
• Final Expenses: ensuring burial costs never become a cash burden on your children.

"Which of these non-Medicare areas is most important for us to address today?"

Steps 3–5 — Confirm Need, Option Sheet & Assumptive Close

Confirm need: "Mr. Smith, after listening to your answers, it seems protecting your savings from out-of-pocket health and recovery expenses is your primary concern. Would you agree?"

Prospect: "Yes, I definitely agree with that."

Solution bridge: "If I could show you a way to eliminate those out-of-pocket financial risks in a manner that fits comfortably within your monthly budget, would that interest you? Let's look at three options on our Option Sheet..."

Assumptive close: "Great decision choosing Option 2, John! In order to get this application started and protect your family, I just need the correct spelling of your beneficiary's full name..." (Stop talking and write on application.)

📞 Missed Call & Phone Prospecting Scripts

Core strategy: dual-channel outreach (voicemail + SMS within 2 minutes increases callback conversion by over 300%). Anchor calls in specific value (Medicare, LTC, Final Expense, Income). Never leave generic "call me back" messages.

1. Immediate Voicemail Script (30 seconds — leave immediately)

"Hi [Client Name], this is Bhupesh Dimri with Bankers Life. I was calling for our scheduled review regarding your [Medicare / Healthcare / Retirement Income] update. I want to ensure you have all the necessary information on upcoming plan options so there are no unexpected out-of-pocket gaps or penalties.

I know you're busy, so no need to rush — I'll send you a quick text with a couple of open times so we can reconnect at your convenience. You can also reach me directly at [Phone Number]. Again, this is Bhupesh with Bankers Life at [Phone Number]. Have a wonderful day!"

2. Immediate Text / SMS Follow-up (send within 2 minutes of voicemail)

"Hi [Client Name], this is Bhupesh Dimri with Bankers Life. Sorry I missed you just now for our quick review regarding your retirement health & income coverage! Would later today at [e.g., 3:15 PM] or tomorrow morning at [e.g., 10:30 AM] work better for a brief 5-minute chat? Let me know what fits your schedule best. Thanks!"

3. The 24-Hour Follow-up Script (second attempt)

"Hi [Client Name], Bhupesh Dimri following up from Bankers Life. I'm calling to make sure we lock in your Annual Policy Review before upcoming plan deadlines. We want to verify that your current setup is providing maximum benefit protection at the lowest out-of-pocket cost.

Please give me a quick call back at [Phone Number], or text me a time that works best for you today. Looking forward to connecting!"

4. Outbound Live Call Script (annual review & lapsed clients)

Agent opening: "Hello [Client Name], this is Bhupesh Dimri with Bankers Life. How are you doing today?"

Value anchor: "I'm reaching out to all my local clients to schedule our annual 4-Square Financial Review. As you know, healthcare guidelines and prescription costs change every year, and my job is to make sure your coverage is still protecting your savings and fitting comfortably in your budget."

Alternative close: "I'll be in your area on [Day] and [Day]. Do mornings or afternoons usually work better for a quick 15-minute kitchen-table review?"

Mandatory compliance & non-endorsement notice: Bankers Life and its licensed agents are not connected with or endorsed by the U.S. government or the federal Medicare program. When conducting Medicare reviews, agents must provide the official Medicare & You handbook / guide. Always adhere to CMS telephonic contact guidelines and Do-Not-Call (DNC) regulations.
